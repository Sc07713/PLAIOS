## My position

The CBA quote is a trap. Drop it. Re-quote with Budget Direct (fresh as new business), AAMI, and RACV. Lift PPOR contents to $80k. Add Hillside building cover. For each line pick the lowest premium where the cover is equivalent and the underwriter has no active claims-handling ASIC litigation. Don't expect a saving — expect total premium to *rise* once Hillside is added and PPOR contents is right-sized. The audit's deliverable is right-sized cover at the best available price, not a saving for its own sake.

## Confidence on each call (self-check)

**Drop CBA/Hollard from the comparison: HIGH confidence.**

Reasoning: ASIC's 25-057MR is not just a complaint — it's a Federal Court action. The fact pattern (18-month delay, refusal to make-safe, property rotted to demolition) is the exact failure mode that an under-buffered family with no liquid emergency fund cannot tolerate on a PPOR. Trustpilot and AFCA backstop this with a pattern rather than a single case. Even if ASIC loses (which is possible — these cases settle or get reduced), the *reason* this household needs insurance is to protect against shock events that exhaust the household's own resources. A claims-handling tail risk is exactly the wrong tail risk to take on a PPOR with $4.5k liquid.

**[uncertain]:** I haven't pulled AFCA's per-insurer complaint statistics. If Hollard's home-claim complaint rate per $1,000 of premium is *not* materially worse than peers, my qualitative argument weakens. I have not verified this.

**Build comparison set as Budget Direct + AAMI + RACV: MEDIUM confidence.**

Reasoning: AAMI is Finder 2026 best-value; RACV is VIC local with 3-year customer-satisfaction streak; Budget Direct re-quoted is the price anchor. Suncorp could substitute for AAMI (same underwriter AAI) but tends to price higher for similar cover. QBE could be added for landlord lines specifically.

**[uncertain]:** I haven't accounted for the fact that *quote pricing varies by individual risk profile*. AAMI being "cheapest of 47 in Finder testing" doesn't mean cheapest for *this* household with 3 properties + a $1.59M debt profile. The right answer depends on how each insurer prices the bundle.

**Lift PPOR contents to $80k: HIGH confidence.**

Reasoning: $20k contents for a 6-person household is structurally inadequate. Standard industry guidance (Canstar, Allianz calculator, NRMA contents calculator) puts a 6-person household at $60–120k. The decision to under-insure contents to save premium is a false economy when the underlying replacement risk hasn't reduced.

**[uncertain]:** I haven't confirmed why the $20k figure exists. If it was a deliberate decision (e.g., they actually have minimal contents because the home is recently built and lightly furnished), the recommendation softens. Worth confirming with user.

**Add Hillside building cover: HIGH confidence.**

Reasoning: $490k of mortgage debt against an uninsured asset is a structural mistake. The cost of cover is bounded; the cost of the asset being destroyed is not.

**Disclose related-party tenancy: HIGH confidence.**

Non-disclosure is grounds for the insurer to void the policy at claim time. The cost of disclosing (potentially loss-of-rent excluded) is much smaller than the cost of having a building claim voided.

**Audit closes on general-insurance scope but flags parked items: HIGH confidence.**

Reasoning: the user explicitly parked life/IP/TPD/trauma. Respect that scope decision. But the underwriting clock on those items doesn't pause because we parked the conversation — surface-and-stop is the right move. The decision record should include a "do not lose this" pointer.

## Where I'm probably wrong

Things I've assumed without verifying:

1. **That Auto & General's ASIC matter is "less severe" than Hollard's.** It's certainly different (pricing transparency vs claims handling), but "millions of customers overcharged for years" might actually be a worse signal than "one home claim in litigation." I've ranked them by my read of consumer impact at claim time; I haven't ranked them by ASIC enforcement priority or by AFCA complaint volume.
2. **That switching insurers is friction-free.** I noted "Budget Direct policies aren't locked in" but I haven't checked whether switching mid-policy carries cancellation fees with Budget Direct specifically. Most Australian general insurance is pro-rata refundable but verify.
3. **That the spec's like-for-like comparison method is sufficient.** Real apples-to-apples on insurance is harder than I've made it look. Excess on motor burnout, sub-limits on jewellery/portable items, definition of "storm" vs "flood," loss-of-rent caps — these all hide premium differences. The 80/20 of what matters is captured but the comparison sheet won't catch every wrinkle.
4. **That a 3-quote comparison is enough.** It's the user-time-realistic answer. A broker would run 6-8. If the spread between top 3 is large, may be worth running a 4th (Suncorp or QBE) to confirm.
