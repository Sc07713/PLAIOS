## Headline finding — re-evaluate the CBA/Hollard switch

**ASIC is actively suing Hollard Insurance** for breach of duty of utmost good faith on a regional Victorian home/contents claim. Hollard delayed the claim 18 months, allowed mould and decay to render the home uninhabitable, then rejected the claim. Federal Court hearing 2026-05-26. Trustpilot 1.3/1.4 stars. AFCA has separately criticised Hollard for "unreasonable" storm-damage delays and "dismissive" handling.

CBA Insurance is *distributed by* CBA but *underwritten by* Hollard (HIP — Hollard Insurance Partners Ltd, formed when Hollard acquired CommInsure from CBA in 2021). So choosing CBA means you're a Hollard customer for claims purposes.

Source: [ASIC media release 25-057MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-057mr-asic-sues-hollard-insurance-alleging-serious-claim-handling-failures/), [Trustpilot Hollard reviews](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/hollard.com.au), [Business News Australia coverage](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/asic-takes-insurer-hollard-to-court-over-alleged-claims-handling-failure.html).

**Implication:** the $1,691/yr "saving" from switching to CBA/Hollard buys you a documented claims-handling problem at the worst possible moment (when you actually need to claim). For owner-occupier home insurance — where the asset is your principal residence and a delayed/denied claim is catastrophic — this is not a saving worth making.

## Related-party landlord tenancy (Hillside)

Tenants are Mina's mother + siblings.

Findings:
- Building/contents cover (landlord-owned structure + landlord contents): generally written normally regardless of tenant relationship.
- **Loss-of-rent / rent-default cover: usually requires a written lease**; many policies exclude or limit cover when tenants are family members because evictions are rarely enforced.
- Tenant damage cover: usually still available with formal lease.
- Insurer-specific PDS reviews required at quote stage.

Source: [Allianz Rent Default cover](https://www.allianz.com.au/home-insurance/rent-default.html), [iSelect Rent Default Insurance](https://www.iselect.com.au/home-insurance/landlord-insurance/rent-default/), [AAMI landlord FAQ](https://www.aami.com.au/faq/will-i-be-covered-by-my-landlord-insurance-if-my-tenant-stops-paying-the-rent-but-does-not-leave-my-property.html).

**Implication for Hillside quote:** focus on building cover and tenant damage cover; treat rent default as bonus rather than central. If rent default *is* available with a formal family lease, that's worth pursuing because the property has $490k of mortgage debt against it that depends on rental income servicing.

## Cost-saving structural levers (independent of insurer choice)

- **Pay annually, not monthly:** typically 10-25% discount.
- **Lift excess to $1,500 or $2,000:** ~10% premium cut per +$500 excess. Constraint here: $4.5k liquid means $2,000 excess starts to bite if a claim occurs. $1,000 looks right.
- **Bundle home + landlord + car with the same insurer:** typically 10-15% combined-policy discount.
- **Avoid the loyalty penalty:** insurers price new business 10-30% below renewal book. Re-quote every 1-2 years even if not switching.
