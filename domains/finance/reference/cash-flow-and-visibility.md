# Cash Flow & Financial Visibility

Before any planning, both partners must see the same picture. Then build systems so the default behaviour produces the right outcome.

---

## Net Worth Snapshot

Single-page view, updated quarterly:

**Assets:**
- Property (current market value, not purchase price)
- Superannuation balances (both partners)
- Investments (shares, managed funds, crypto, other)
- Cash/savings
- Business equity (conservative valuation — not aspirational)
- Vehicles and other significant assets

**Liabilities:**
- Mortgage(s) (balance, rate, type, offset)
- Personal loans
- Credit cards (balances carried, not limits)
- Business-related personal guarantees
- HECS/HELP debt
- Any other obligations

**Net worth:** Assets minus liabilities. Track quarterly for trajectory, not just position.

---

## Household Cash Flow Map

Monthly view:

**Income streams:**
- Primary employment/drawings (both partners)
- Business distributions (dividends, trust, salary — specify structure)
- Investment income
- Government payments/benefits
- Any other recurring income

**Fixed obligations:**
- Mortgage/rent, insurance premiums, school fees, childcare, utilities (averaged), subscriptions, loan repayments, minimum super contributions

**Variable essentials:**
- Groceries, fuel/transport, medical/dental, clothing (kids grow — this is essential, not discretionary), home maintenance

**Discretionary:**
- Dining out, entertainment, hobbies, personal spending (each partner), holidays, gifts

**Savings/investment:**
- Whatever is systematically directed to wealth building — not "what's left over"

**Key rule:** If savings is "what's left over," savings will be zero. Savings must be a fixed obligation, not a residual.

---

## 30-Day Awareness Exercise

For couples who genuinely don't know where money goes: both partners track every dollar for 30 days. No judgment, no budgeting — pure observation. The goal is shared data, not behaviour change (that comes later). Use a shared app, spreadsheet, or physical notebook. The output is raw material for system design.

## Account Architecture

Recommended structure (adapt to available accounts):

| Account | Purpose | Funded How |
|---|---|---|
| **Joint Operating** | All household bills, fixed obligations, groceries, shared expenses | All income lands here first |
| **Joint Savings / Offset** | Emergency fund, short-term goals, mortgage offset | Automatic transfer on payday — fixed amount, not percentage of "leftovers" |
| **Partner A Personal** | Discretionary spending — no questions asked | Automatic transfer on payday |
| **Partner B Personal** | Discretionary spending — no questions asked | Automatic transfer on payday |
| **Investment / Wealth Building** | Long-term wealth accumulation beyond emergency fund | Automatic transfer after savings target met |
| **Tax Provisioning** (if applicable) | For business owners — set aside estimated tax obligations | Percentage of business drawings |

**Critical design principle:** Income flows into the joint operating account. Savings, personal spending, and investment are automatically swept *out* on payday before discretionary spending begins. What remains in the operating account is genuinely available to spend. This removes the willpower problem entirely.

## Savings Rate Targets

Work with a household savings rate rather than arbitrary dollar figures:

| Savings Rate | Assessment | Trajectory |
|---|---|---|
| < 5% | **Critical** — wealth not being built, likely going backwards after inflation | Emergency review needed |
| 5-10% | **Minimum viable** — building slowly, vulnerable to shocks | Acceptable short-term if debt reduction is active |
| 10-20% | **Healthy** — consistent wealth accumulation | Target range for most households |
| 20-30% | **Strong** — accelerated timeline to financial goals | Appropriate for high-income, exit-planning households |
| 30%+ | **Aggressive** — rapid wealth building | Sustainable only if lifestyle needs are genuinely met |

**Savings rate = (Total saved + invested + extra debt repayment) / Gross household income x 100**

Track monthly. Review quarterly. The trend matters more than any single month.

## Expense Guardrails

Category guardrails rather than line-item budgets (which feel controlling and rarely stick):

- **Housing costs** (mortgage/rent + rates + insurance + maintenance): Target <= 30% of gross household income. Risk trigger: > 35%.
- **Transport:** Target <= 10%. Multiple vehicles in a single-income household should be scrutinised.
- **Lifestyle discretionary** (dining, entertainment, hobbies, personal): Target <= 15%. This is where lifestyle creep hides.
- **Children** (education, activities, clothing, childcare): Highly variable — no universal target, but should be consciously chosen, not default-accumulated.

**Guardrails, not handcuffs.** Exceeding a guardrail in a given month isn't a failure — it's a signal to investigate. Three consecutive months over a guardrail is a pattern that needs a decision.
