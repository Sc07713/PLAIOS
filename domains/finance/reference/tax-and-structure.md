# Tax & Structure Optimisation

The gap between gross income and after-tax household cash flow can be enormous — and is often the most accessible lever for increasing savings without reducing lifestyle. This layer is especially critical for business owners.

---

## Business Owner Extraction Strategy

How income moves from the business to the household matters significantly:

- **Salary/wages:** Simple, predictable, generates super guarantee entitlements. But fully taxed at marginal rate.
- **Dividends:** Tax-effective if franking credits available. But doesn't generate super.
- **Trust distributions:** Flexible — can split income to lower-tax partner. But requires appropriate structure and compliance.
- **Combination:** Most business owners benefit from a blended approach optimised annually with their accountant.

**The advisor's role:** Flag when the current extraction method appears suboptimal and recommend a review with the accountant. This is not tax advice — it's pattern recognition.

---

## Superannuation Optimisation

Super is the most tax-advantaged savings vehicle available in Australia. Common missed opportunities:

- Concessional contributions up to the cap ($30,000/year as of 2024-25) — particularly for business owners who often underpay themselves super
- Spouse contributions for non-working or lower-income partners
- Carry-forward unused concessional cap from prior years (up to 5 years)
- Salary sacrifice arrangements for the employed partner
- Reviewing super fund performance, fees, and insurance within super

---

## Deduction & Offset Awareness

Maintain a running list of potential deductions and offsets. Common categories for business owner households:

- Home office expenses
- Professional development
- Income protection insurance premiums
- Investment property costs
- Charitable donations
- Private health insurance rebate review

**Annual rhythm:** Pre-EOFY review (May-June) to identify and action opportunities before the year closes. Post-EOFY debrief with accountant to capture lessons for next year.

## Investment Property — Repair vs Improvement Rule of Thumb

The single biggest deduction-classification question when spending on an investment property.

### The equation

```
Tax refund  =  Deduction amount  ×  Marginal tax rate (+ Medicare levy)
```

For Scott/Mina in the 30% bracket + 2% Medicare = **32% effective rate**.

### Repair vs improvement — where the real money is

| Nature of work | When deductible | Tax impact on $10K spend |
|---|---|---|
| **Repair** — restores original function (patch roof, replace broken tiles, fix leak) | 100% in the year spent | **$3,200 refund that year** |
| **Improvement / Capital works** — new roof, renovation, upgrade | 2.5%/year over 40 years | **$80/year refund** for 40 years |
| **Depreciable item** (Div 40 — appliances, carpets, HWS, solar) | Over item's effective life (e.g. 10-20 years) | Varies — faster than capital works |

### Rule of thumb

> **For every $1 spent on a repair to an investment property, the ATO gives you ~32¢ back at tax time.**
>
> For an improvement, you get the same 32¢ — but trickled over 40 years.

### The three ATO tests (substance, not invoice label)

1. **Does it restore original function?** → Repair (immediate deduction)
2. **Does it go beyond original, or replace something wholesale?** → Capital works / depreciable
3. **Was the damage pre-existing when you acquired the property or first rented it?** → Initial repair (NOT immediately deductible — becomes capital works)

### Common real-world patterns

- Replacing half the tiles that blew off in a storm → **repair**
- Replacing the entire roof because it's old → **capital works** (improvement, even if old-for-new)
- Adding colorbond over tiles → **capital works** (improvement)
- Fixing leak damage to ceiling plaster → **repair**
- Replacing a failed hot water system with equivalent → **Div 40 depreciable item**
- Upgrading to a premium HWS → **capital works**

### Practical implication

Accountants earn their fees on the classification call, not the arithmetic. The substance test matters more than the invoice heading. When commissioning work, consider asking tradies to itemise the invoice between repair and improvement components where genuinely both.
