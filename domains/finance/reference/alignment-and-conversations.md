# Alignment & Conversations

This is the layer most frameworks skip. It's the most important one.

---

## Values Mapping

Each partner independently answers:

1. What does financial security mean to me? (Not a number — a feeling. "Knowing we could survive 6 months without income" is different from "owning our home outright.")
2. What am I willing to sacrifice for long-term goals? What am I not willing to sacrifice?
3. What spending do I consider essential that my partner might consider discretionary? (Be honest.)
4. What's my biggest financial fear?
5. What's my financial aspiration for 5 years from now? 10 years?

Then compare answers. The gaps are the negotiation points. The overlaps are the foundation.

---

## Priority Negotiation

Using the values mapping, agree on a ranked priority list. Common categories:

- Emergency fund (target: 3-6 months of fixed obligations)
- Debt reduction (which debts, in what order)
- Children's education (public vs private, university fund)
- Retirement/super optimisation
- Property (PPOR upgrade, investment property, paying down mortgage)
- Lifestyle (holidays, home improvements, vehicle upgrades)
- Business investment/growth
- Charitable giving

**The rule:** Both partners must agree on the top 3. Not "I'll let you have yours if I get mine" — genuine shared priority. Everything below the top 3 is secondary and funded only after the top 3 are on track.

---

## Spending Autonomy Agreement

Each partner gets a defined amount of personal discretionary spending per month — no questions asked, no judgment, no reporting required. This prevents the "permission culture" that destroys financial partnerships. The amount is agreed jointly and reviewed quarterly.

---

## The Monthly Money Meeting

Structure for the monthly 30-minute review. Keep it neutral and data-driven — this is a business meeting about the household, not a judgment session.

1. **Open with wins** (2 min): What went well? Saved target, paid off a debt, resisted impulse purchase, found a better deal.
2. **Cash flow review** (10 min): Actual vs guardrails by category. Identify patterns or one-offs.
3. **Savings rate check** (5 min): On track? If not, what changed and is it temporary or structural?
4. **Upcoming month** (10 min): Known large expenses, upcoming decisions, anything needing agreement.
5. **Close with alignment check** (3 min): Both comfortable with where things stand? Anything unspoken?

**Ground rules:** No blame language ("you spent..."), no historical grievances ("you always..."), no unilateral decisions announced as fait accompli. Both partners speak. Both partners listen.

---

## The Difficult Conversation Starter

For genuine disagreements about a financial decision:

*"I want to talk about [specific thing]. My concern is [specific concern]. What I'd like is [specific outcome]. What's your perspective?"*

This prevents the spiral of vague complaints -> defensive reactions -> argument -> nothing changes.

## Cross-Domain Interfaces

### Business Finance
- Business extraction strategy directly affects household income — changes in one require review of the other
- Personal guarantees on business debt are household liabilities, not just business ones
- Business valuation is a household asset — but illiquid and uncertain. Don't plan retirement around an unsold business

### Tax & Accounting
- Household tax strategy and business tax strategy must be coordinated, not optimised independently
- Accountant should see the full picture — personal and business — annually
- Super contributions, trust distributions, and spouse strategies require professional input

### Estate & Legal
- Business succession planning and personal estate planning are interdependent
- Shareholder agreements, buy-sell arrangements, and key person insurance affect both domains
