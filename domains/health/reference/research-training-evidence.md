# Training Research Evidence Compilation
*Consolidated findings from multiple research documents*

---

## HIT-Hybrid Upper/Lower Split Evidence

### Frequency (Volume-Equated)
- Schoenfeld 2019 (25 studies, 2,058 participants): No significant difference between frequencies when volume equated (p>0.05)
- Ralston 2018: Trivial ES of 0.03 comparing frequencies in volume-equated conditions
- Pelland 2025: 100% posterior probability hypertrophy increases with volume; frequency effects negligible when volume equated

### Volume
- Optimal: 12-20 weekly sets per muscle group (Baz-Valle 2022)
- Each additional weekly set increases hypertrophy ~0.37% (Schoenfeld 2017)
- Krieger 2010: Multiple sets = 40% greater hypertrophy than single sets (ES 0.24 vs 0.34 vs 0.44)

### Failure Training
- Robinson/Steele 2024: Strength similar across wide RIR range; hypertrophy improved closer to failure
- Grgic 2021: No significant overall difference (ES 0.22, CI crossing zero)
- Refalo 2024: Similar quad hypertrophy between failure and 1-2 RIR over 8 weeks
- CNS fatigue largely overstated -- resolves 48-72 hours (Thomas 2018)
- Mixed approach: compounds at 2-3 RIR, final isolation sets to failure

### Tempo
- Schoenfeld 2015: Rep durations 0.5-8s produce similar hypertrophy; **>10s is inferior**
- 6/6 tempo (12s total) falls in inferior range -- requires load reduction to 40-60% 1RM
- Schuenke: Traditional tempo = 38.8% fiber area increase vs 10.6% for super-slow
- Optimal: 3-4s eccentric + 1-2s concentric (Wilk 2021)

### Load
- Schoenfeld 2017 (21 studies): No significant hypertrophy difference between low-load (<=60% 1RM) and high-load (>60% 1RM) when sets taken to failure

### Recovery After 40
- 2020 systematic review: Only 4 of 11 studies found worse recovery in older adults
- Anabolic resistance: ~40g protein needed (vs 20g in young) to maximize MPS
- Testosterone: 1-3% decline/year after 40, compensated by training stimulus
- Allow 48-72 hours between same muscle groups

## Longevity Sweet Spot
- Copenhagen City Heart Study (8,697 adults, 25+ years): Optimal 2.6-4.5 hours/week
- Mortality risk increases beyond 10 hours/week (HR: 1.22)
- Resistance training benefits peak at 30-60 minutes/week
- Intensity > volume for mortality reduction (41% CVD mortality reduction)

---

## HIIT vs Steps Evidence
- 15-20 min HIIT = equivalent or superior to 10,000 steps (~2 hours)
- 6-8x time efficiency ratio
- EPOC: metabolism elevated 24-48 hours post-HIIT vs minimal from walking
- 25% greater VO2 max improvements than moderate continuous training
- SIT: 40% better fat reduction than traditional HIIT in 60% less time
- Minimum effective dose: 4.4 min/day vigorous activity = 26-30% all-cause mortality reduction (UK Biobank, 25,241 participants)
- Gibala "One Minute Workout": 3x20s all-out = 150 min moderate exercise benefits
- Benefits plateau around 7,500-9,000 steps for mortality reduction

## Testosterone and Exercise
- Evening exercise (16:30-20:00): up to 84% more muscle mass than morning (24-week study, 42 men)
- Circadian: natural testosterone peaks AM but greater exercise responsiveness PM
- Compound exercises: 20-30% greater testosterone response than isolation
- HIIT: 36.7% testosterone increase after 8 weeks (men 35-40)
- 1 week of 5-hour sleep = 10-15% testosterone decrease
- Complete training 2-4 hours before bed to protect sleep architecture
- Exercise snacks (2-3 min) throughout day maintain metabolic activity

## Key Protocols Referenced
- Tabata: 4 min (8x 20s work/10s rest) = 15% VO2max + 28% anaerobic capacity (6 weeks)
- Norwegian 4x4: 28 min total, 13% VO2max improvement (8 weeks)
- REHIT: 2x20s all-out efforts = significant adaptations
- Kettlebell swings: 20 cal/min (highest of any exercise studied)
